core_principles:

  - id: CP07
    principle: "Specialization works best in kind environments."
    explanation: >
      Domains with stable rules, repetitive patterns,
      rapid feedback, and clear metrics reward deliberate repetition.
    examples:
      - chess
      - golf
      - firefighting under familiar conditions
    characteristics:
      - repeatability
      - immediate feedback
      - low ambiguity
      - stable rules

  - id: CP08
    principle: "Wicked environments punish narrow expertise."
    explanation: >
      In environments where feedback is noisy, delayed,
      incomplete, or changing, experience alone does not guarantee improvement.
    examples:
      - markets
      - geopolitics
      - macroeconomics
      - hiring
      - forecasting
      - medicine
    implication:
      - experience can reinforce wrong beliefs.

  - id: CP09
    principle: "Experience without valid feedback creates illusion of expertise."
    explanation: >
      Confidence may increase while actual predictive ability stagnates.
    examples:
      - financial forecasting
      - military assessments
      - hiring decisions
      - political prediction

  - id: CP10
    principle: "Systems optimized locally may fail globally."
    explanation: >
      Specialized silos optimize their own metrics while damaging system-level outcomes.
    examples:
      - banking crisis
      - medical overtreatment
      - organizational fragmentation

  - id: CP11
    principle: "Breadth improves strategic thinking."
    explanation: >
      Tactical execution can be automated or outsourced,
      increasing the relative value of strategic synthesis.
    examples:
      - freestyle chess
      - AI-assisted decision systems
      - interdisciplinary innovation

  - id: CP12
    principle: "Automation shifts advantage from execution to abstraction."
    explanation: >
      As machines master repeatable tactical tasks,
      humans gain comparative advantage through:
        - judgment
        - synthesis
        - adaptation
        - framing problems
        - strategy

  - id: CP13
    principle: "Cross-domain exposure prevents trench thinking."
    explanation: >
      Innovation often emerges when people escape domain silos
      and integrate concepts across boundaries.

operational_rules:

  trading_rules:

    - rule: "Treat markets as wicked environments."
      checklist:
        - Is feedback delayed?
        - Could this outcome be luck?
        - Is the regime changing?
        - Am I overfitting recent data?

    - rule: "Separate signal from narrative."
      implementation:
        - Require statistical validation.
        - Use long sample sizes.
        - Compare across regimes.
        - Maintain hypothesis logs.

    - rule: "Do not infer causality from recent profits."
      checklist:
        - Was edge structural or temporary?
        - Did volatility conditions change?
        - Was liquidity abnormal?
        - Was this survivorship bias?

    - rule: "Exploration belongs in sandbox."
      implementation:
        - Dedicated research portfolio.
        - Explicit experimentation budget.
        - No production contamination.

  machine_learning_rules:

    - rule: "Optimize for generalization, not training accuracy."
      checklist:
        - Does model survive unseen data?
        - Is validation diverse?
        - Is this regime dependent?
        - Is feature importance stable?

    - rule: "Avoid narrow data dependence."
      implementation:
        - Use multiple market regimes.
        - Use stress testing.
        - Use adversarial validation.

  decision_rules:

    - rule: "Confidence must be externally validated."
      checklist:
        - What is base rate?
        - What evidence would falsify this?
        - Am I relying on intuition alone?
        - Is feedback trustworthy?

    - rule: "Maintain strategic perspective."
      implementation:
        - Zoom out regularly.
        - Review system-wide effects.
        - Avoid silo optimization.

    The deeper thesis emerging from Range is that specialization is highly effective
    only in environments where reality behaves predictably.

    In dynamic systems — markets, technology, organizations, careers —
    breadth, adaptability, probabilistic thinking,
    and interdisciplinary synthesis become dominant advantages.

    The future increasingly rewards:
      - strategic integrators
      - adaptive learners
      - cross-domain thinkers
      - meta-system designers
