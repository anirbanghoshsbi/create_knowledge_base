core_principles:

  - id: CP01
    principle: "Early specialization is not universally optimal."
    explanation: >
      Early focused training creates short-term advantages,
      but broader exploration often creates superior long-term adaptability,
      creativity, resilience, and transfer ability.
    evidence:
      - Tiger Woods represents early specialization.
      - Roger Federer represents delayed specialization and broad sampling.
      - Research across sports shows elites often specialized later.
    implications:
      - Exploration is not wasted time.
      - Delayed commitment may improve fit and mastery.
      - Breadth can outperform narrow optimization in complex domains.

  - id: CP02
    principle: "Sampling periods improve future expertise."
    explanation: >
      Exposure to multiple domains builds transferable mental models,
      movement patterns, heuristics, and adaptive thinking.
    mechanisms:
      - transfer learning
      - abstraction
      - pattern recognition
      - analogical reasoning
    implication:
      - Diverse experiences compound nonlinearly later.

  - id: CP03
    principle: "The most effective learning often appears inefficient."
    explanation: >
      Deep learning is slower, harder, and less immediately rewarding.
      Fast apparent progress often reflects shallow pattern memorization.
    related_concepts:
      - desirable difficulty
      - delayed fluency
      - long-term retention
      - adaptive struggle

  - id: CP04
    principle: "Complex environments reward adaptability over rigidity."
    explanation: >
      In environments with uncertainty, novelty, and changing rules,
      narrow optimization eventually breaks.
    examples:
      - modern careers
      - technology
      - investing
      - entrepreneurship
      - military transitions

  - id: CP05
    principle: "Breadth increases creative recombination."
    explanation: >
      Innovation frequently emerges from combining ideas across domains.
    mechanism:
      - cross-domain analogy
      - conceptual transfer
      - outsider perspective
    examples:
      - inventors
      - artists
      - polymaths

  - id: CP06
    principle: "Linear career progression is overrated."
    explanation: >
      Nonlinear trajectories may produce stronger eventual alignment
      between abilities, interests, and opportunities.
    implication:
      - Career switching can be strategic rather than failure.

    assumption: "The environment remains stable long enough for specialization to dominate."

    reality: >
      Early leaders frequently plateau due to rigidity and burnout.

    assumption: "Efficiency during learning implies better learning."

      Over-specialization often reduces adaptability and innovation.

operational_rules:

  trading_rules:

    - rule: "Protect core profitable systems from exploratory impulses."
      implementation:
        - Maintain separate sandbox capital.
        - Never modify live strategy without forward testing.
        - Require statistical evidence before deployment.

    - rule: "Exploration must be isolated."
      checklist:
        - Is this production or sandbox?
        - Has this been backtested?
        - Has this survived multiple regimes?
        - Am I changing because of boredom?

    - rule: "Preserve regime adaptability."
      implementation:
        - Study multiple market structures.
        - Maintain macro awareness.
        - Avoid single-indicator dependency.

  learning_rules:

    - rule: "Prioritize transfer over memorization."
      checklist:
        - Can this idea apply across domains?
        - What is the invariant principle?
        - What assumptions does this depend on?

    - rule: "Deliberately create conceptual collisions."
      implementation:
        - Read outside primary domain weekly.
        - Connect unrelated fields.
        - Maintain idea graph/network.

    - rule: "Accept slow learning."
      reminders:
        - Confusion is not failure.
        - Retrieval difficulty strengthens memory.
        - Immediate fluency is unreliable.

  decision_rules:

    - rule: "Delay irreversible specialization."
      checklist:
        - Have I explored alternatives?
        - Am I locking into identity too early?
        - Is this optimization local or global?

    - rule: "Maintain optionality."
      implementation:
        - Build transferable skills.
        - Avoid single-point dependency.
        - Preserve flexibility in systems and career.

meta_insight:

  summary: >
    The central insight of Range is not merely that breadth matters.
    It is that complex adaptive environments reward systems and individuals
    capable of transfer, recombination, delayed specialization,
    and flexible adaptation.

    Specialization dominates stable environments.
    Range dominates evolving environments.
